"look into why children modules are also firing it directly in front when they should

## 3. Root-cause candidate (the actual gap)
activationAngle is a DEAD ASSIGNMENT:
  declared line 119 (private var activationAngle = ship.facing)
  written  line 202 (activationAngle = evaluationResult.angle)
  read     NOWHERE (grep confirms only those two lines).
ChildModuleAimingEvaluator sweeps 360 degrees (sweepingStep) and returns the best angle,
which is stored and then DISCARDED. onActivate()/showVisualFlair() pulls toward
getFacingToTarget() — the ship's CURRENT AI target (typically ahead), not the best sweep
direction. Hence "children fire directly in front."

## 4. Investigation steps
1. Confirm intended consumption: should activationAngle drive the showVisualFlair() arc
   direction + pull direction for child (non-root) modules? (Root is forced straight ahead
   per the showVisualFlair comment.)
2. Verify current-target direction diverges from sweep result observably — i.e. the gap is
   real, not cosmetic.
3. Decide fix shape: wire activationAngle into the child branch of showVisualFlair()/pull;
   or replace getFacingToTarget() with activationAngle for children.
4. Pure-code boundary: extract the sweep scoring into a decoupled, unit-testable function
   (TDD boundary) so angle selection is provable; wire it in.

## 5. Verification approach
- Unit tests for the extracted angle-selection logic (pure, no engine deps).
- Baseline: post-strip Windows build; reproduce child firing straight ahead with a clear
  off-angle target nearby; after fix: fires toward the sweep-picked angle.
- Re-run with JAR rebuilt (Windows-side).

## 6. Deliverables
